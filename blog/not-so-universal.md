---
title: 'Not so universal'
description: 'A rant not even about electrical standards or protocols'
pubDate: 'June 16 2026'
---

My bike light is a Serfas True 1000 Road Headlight. I really like the light for being a light, but the thing that drives me a bit mad is literally the only port on it. It charges via USB-C which is great, since I strongly believe that all my devices should just charge over USB-C (looking at you Kindle Oasis, my last micro-usb device), this device has the weird property that **90% of the cables I own don't fit into the port**.

I know the USB-C spec is a little loosey-goosey when it comes to cables that look like they should work but not working protocol- or electrically-wise, but does the spec say anything about the required clearance on all sides of the port? It is wild to me that the cables I have literally cannot reach the port because of physical obstructions. It seems like nearly all cable makers have come to the same conclusion about how thick their strain relief connectors should be, but I guess some device manufacturers didn't get the memo.

I have two usb c cables that seem to fit. The one I normally use I actually had repurposed a while back as my webcam cable and is now pretty well tangled up with all the other cables running to my monitor. Luckily, I had a fresh still-wrapped cable that came with some other device and it seems it also has small enough walls on the connector that it can clear the physical constraints of the flashlight and make it to the charging port. Still, I tried close to 8 different cables that all didn't fit before finding this one.

Serfas, if you're reading this, please try the next headlight you make with a handful of cables on the market and make sure that they all fit. 

2 out of 5 stars. The light is great and has lasted for me for years, but every time I have to charge this thing I'm digging through my cables to see what fits.
